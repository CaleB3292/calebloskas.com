How I lead

Clarity, energy, results.

Three principles I run a team on. Every rep should know where they stand, want to be in the room, and see the work show up in the numbers.

01

Clarity

Clear expectations on pipeline, forecast, deal quality and development. I use rep profiles to understand each person's strengths, gaps, goals and motivation, so nobody has to guess what needs to improve.

02

Energy

Lead from the front and build a team people want to be part of. Celebrate wins, keep reps connected through change and pressure, and coach with care while still giving direct feedback when standards aren't met.

03

Results

A repeatable rhythm of 1:1s, deal reviews, call reviews and forecast inspection. Stay close to key deals, catch risk early, and measure progress in attainment, pipeline, forecast accuracy and rep growth.

The operating system

Four habits that build consistent reps.

Rep development

Weeks 1–4 ramp milestones, daily prospecting blocks, weekly early-stage pipeline reviews, and a personal review of each new rep's first 10 calls and first 5 demos.

Coaching

Biweekly 1:1s with a set agenda, two to three recorded calls reviewed per rep each week, and weekly strategy sessions on the top 10 deals. Real deals, never hypotheticals.

Operational excellence

Find the top three friction points in the first 30 days, standardize deal reviews, pipeline hygiene and forecast criteria, and work with sales ops to cut admin time.

Performance management

Clear weekly KPIs for pipeline created, meetings booked and deal progression, tracked every week, with an intervention plan within two weeks of a slip.

Coaching playbook

Different reps need different coaching.

I start every coaching plan with one diagnostic question, then pick the approach that fits the person.

The top performer

Is the success repeatable, healthy and forecastable?

Give autonomy, inspect quality, and challenge them to raise the standard for the whole team.

The lone wolf

Is independence creating leverage or cultural drag?

Connect influence to legacy. The next level isn't just being the best rep, so set clear expectations around team contribution.

20% at mid-quarter

Is this pipeline, skill, will, territory or execution?

Build a recovery plan for this quarter and a separate development plan for the long run.

High skill, low motivation

Is this temporary or becoming a pattern? What changed?

A direct conversation about the root cause, whether burnout, goals, comp or something personal. Then reset expectations and watch for the change.

The new hire

Are they building the right habits early?

Clear ramp milestones, call reviews, pipeline creation and a lot of confidence building.

My plan for the team

My first year as manager.

Days 0–30

Understand what drives performance.

Build a profile for every rep: strengths, gaps, motivation, career goals and coaching style. Study pipeline health and conversion, find what separates top performers, and land one or two quick wins per rep.

Days 31–60

Drive consistency.

Weekly coaching on pipeline quality and deal progression, standard workflows for pipeline and forecasting, team-wide training where gaps are shared, and targeted plans where they aren't.

Days 61–90

Scale what works.

A culture of ownership and accountability. Lift top performers while coaching everyone else upward, and push for 3–4x pipeline coverage, better conversion and higher attainment.

Year one

A predictable, high-accountability team.

A team known for reliable forecasts, strong culture and developing talent, with systems that grow reps without making the manager the bottleneck.
